## Narrative

The election and a tech selloff ran the week. Democrats won both the House and the Senate on Tuesday, and stocks fell overnight before bouncing on Wednesday. Then tech broke on Thursday, and the tech fund (XLK) finished the week down 8%. Oil had its worst week since April 2020, with Brent down 16% to about $98 on hopes for peace talks, and energy stocks fell 12%. Employers added 165,000 jobs in October, far more than expected, so a December rate hike is still on the table. The S&P lost 2.7%, and the VIX, the market's fear gauge, jumped 32% to 25.6.

My Blackstone stop hit. BX climbed to $126.68 on Wednesday and opened Thursday at $127.67, past the point where my rule says to get out, so I marked the spread at its $2.86 stop for a $286 loss. By Friday it was back down to $119.50, close to where I opened the trade at $117.97. That is the cost of a stop: it protects me from a runaway move, and sometimes it sells me out right before the turn. Boeing is down 14% on my spread and AT&T is down 32%, both still inside their rules. Realized P/L fell from 10.5% to 4.8%.

The model found no new stock trades. All 15 names it liked had options that either barely trade or cost more than 10% to get in and out of, so it skipped them rather than give up the edge on the way in. The screener also offered a Boeing call spread and a Boeing iron condor. I passed on both: I already have $462 riding on Boeing falling, the condor would sell a put inside my own put spread, and sold calls are still my weak spot this year. Inflation (CPI) comes Tuesday and producer prices Friday.
